The guide

The Gap Between What You Are Worth And What Your Market Can Find

A local search guide for private practice physical therapy and chiropractic owners. Long, specific, and written so you could run every bit of it yourself.

See Where You Actually Rank→

You did not open a practice because you love marketing. You opened it because you can take somebody who cannot lift their child and give them their life back.

And yet here you are, wondering why the schedule has holes in it you cannot explain, while a clinic four miles away with worse clinicians and a better listing is turning people away.

Here is the thing almost nobody will say to you plainly. You are already sitting on an enormous asset that is producing nothing. Your expertise. Your outcomes. The reputation you have built one patient at a time. None of it is visible to the person who, at this moment, is typing their pain into a search bar looking for somebody exactly like you.

That gap, between what you are genuinely worth and what your market can actually find, is the most expensive thing in your practice. It does not appear on any statement. It has no line item. And closing it is almost certainly the highest-return work available to you that does not involve treating another patient.

This guide is about closing it.

One

Why Local Search, And Not Something Else

Stop asking what a marketing channel costs. Start asking what it leaves behind when you stop paying for it.

Paid ads are cut flowers

Beautiful the moment you buy them and dead the moment you stop. The traffic ends with the invoice. There is nothing wrong with cut flowers, and we run them for practices where they make sense, but understand what you are buying.

Directories are rented land

Useful, sometimes very useful, and entirely outside your control. Their pricing changes, their algorithm changes, and your visibility changes with it. You do not own the ground you are standing on.

Referrals are real but unpredictable

You cannot forecast next quarter on somebody remembering your name. The physician referral model has been thinning for years, which is precisely why you are reading this.

Local search is the garden

It takes a season to establish, and once it produces, it keeps producing. More importantly: you own it outright. No bid, no membership, no landlord. Every improvement is a permanent addition to the balance sheet of the practice.

Stop renting your growth and start owning it.

Why you are held to a higher standard, and why that helps you

Google treats healthcare sites differently. The classification is YMYL, for "Your Money or Your Life," and choosing a clinician for your back plainly qualifies. Anything that could meaningfully affect somebody's health gets extra scrutiny before it ranks.

So Google looks for evidence: who you are, what you are qualified to do, whether the practice is real and established. The framework is E-E-A-T, for Experience, Expertise, Authoritativeness and Trustworthiness.

Here is the part that should relieve you. The work that earns Google's trust is the same work that earns a nervous patient's trust. Real credentials, plainly displayed. A photograph of an actual clinician. Clear answers to the questions people are too embarrassed to ask. Evidence that other people came here and got better.

You are not doing two jobs. You are doing one job that pays twice.

The opportunity nobody in your market is taking

Most PT and chiropractic websites are badly neglected. That is a shame for the profession and a gift to you.

The bar to outrank the practice down the street is far lower than you think. You do not need to be excellent at this. You need to be slightly better than people who are barely trying, and sustained slightly-better compounds into a lead that becomes very hard to close.

How long, honestly

Three to six months for meaningful traction. Real compounding after a year. Some things move in days, like claiming a Google Business Profile that was never claimed, or fixing a title tag that says "Home."

Anybody promising you a number one ranking in thirty days is telling you something important about how they operate. Google itself warns you about them.

Two

The Starving Crowd: The Words Your Future Patients Already Use

There is an old question in marketing. If you could have one advantage over every competing restaurant, what would you choose? The location, the chef, the concept?

The right answer is: a starving crowd.

Who you reach matters more than what you say to them. And the extraordinary thing about local search is that the crowd has already identified itself. Somebody typing "sciatica treatment near me" at eleven at night is not browsing. They are in pain, they have decided to do something about it, and they are choosing between you and whoever else appears.

Your only job is to be there when they look.

The four mistakes that cost the most

  • Speaking clinician instead of patient. You say "manual lymphatic drainage." She types "swelling after surgery." You say "vestibular rehabilitation." He types "dizzy when I stand up." Meet them in their words, not yours. Their words are the ones in the search bar.
  • Chasing terms nobody can own. "Physical therapy" is not a keyword, it is a category. It tells Google nothing about where you are or what you fix. Use broad terms as context, never as targets.
  • Ignoring location entirely. Your patients are within a few miles of your door. Without location terms you are competing against the entire country for people who will never visit you.
  • Burying insurance. The most expensive omission on this list, and almost everybody makes it.

The five categories that actually matter

  • 01

    Discipline plus location

    Your foundation. "Physical therapist in Arlington." "Chiropractor Falls Church." Every practice must own these.

  • 02

    Specialty plus need

    What makes you the obvious choice rather than an option. "Pelvic floor PT near me." "Sports chiropractor for runners." "Hand therapy after surgery." The narrower your claim, the easier you are to find and the less you compete on price.

  • 03

    Conditions and symptoms

    How people actually search, which is by how they feel rather than by diagnosis. "Lower back pain that will not go away." "Shoulder pain after rotator cuff surgery." "Numbness in hand at night."

  • 04

    Insurance

    The highest intent of anything on this page. Somebody searching "PT that takes Blue Cross near me" has already decided they want treatment and is now filtering for who they can afford. They convert better than any other search, and most practices hide this information on a single page nobody reads.

    • Name every carrier you accept. Never write "most major insurances."
    • Put it on each service page, not one buried page.
    • Name Medicare and Medicaid specifically if you take them.
    • Do not forget workers' compensation and auto injury. Those are urgent and high-intent.
  • 05

    Questions

    "How many PT sessions after a knee replacement?" "Do I need a referral for pelvic floor therapy?" "Is a chiropractor safe during pregnancy?" These build authority rather than book appointments directly, and they matter more than they look. Section five explains why.

The long tail is where a small practice wins

"Chiropractor" is a fight you will not win and would not enjoy winning. Mixed intent, enormous competition, poor conversion.

"Prenatal chiropractor in Arlington VA" is a fight you can win this year. Fewer searches, almost all of them ready to book, and the practices competing for it are not paying attention.

Long-tail terms are less competitive, which means you do not need a decade-old domain or a large budget to own them. That is the entire opening for a practice your size.

Free tools, today

  • Google itself. Autocomplete, "People Also Ask," and the related searches at the bottom of the page. That is real demand data, free.
  • Google Search Console. Shows the exact queries already reaching you, including terms you rank eleventh for and could rank fifth for.
  • Ahrefs or Semrush when you are ready to invest. Both have free tiers worth using first.

Fifteen minutes, and you will have more strategy than most of your competitors

Take your three most important services. For each, write three lines.

Discipline plus city. Specialty plus city. One symptom a patient would type, in their words, plus city.

That is nine keywords, and it is a strategy.

Three

Making Your Website An Asset You Own

Your site is the only piece of this nobody can take from you. Treat it accordingly.

The basics that move the needle

Every page is a separate chance to rank for one specific thing.

  • Title tag. The clickable headline in results. Primary term, location, practice name, under 60 characters. "Pelvic Floor Physical Therapy Arlington VA | Practice Name" beats "Home" by a distance that is difficult to overstate.
  • Meta description. Not a ranking factor, but it decides whether anybody clicks, and clicks are a signal. 150 to 160 characters. Write it as an invitation, not a summary.
  • Headings. Exactly one H1, containing your primary term. H2s and H3s to organise and to work in related language.
  • URLs. Short and descriptive. A path that reads as words beats a path that reads as a database key.
  • Image alt text. "Physical therapist working with patient on shoulder mobility in Arlington clinic" beats a camera filename.
  • Internal links. Point related pages at each other. It shows Google how your practice is organised and it walks a visitor toward booking.

The pages every practice needs

Use this as an audit. Each one should be earning its place.

  • 01

    Homepage

    Who you are, what you fix, where you are, within five seconds. Primary term and a booking action visible without scrolling.

  • 02

    One page per service

    The highest-impact structural move available to you, and the one most practices skip. "Pediatric Chiropractic in Arlington VA" can rank. A single page called "Services" cannot. Each page needs the service, the conditions it treats, your approach, what the first visit is actually like, the insurances accepted, and a way to book.

  • 03

    About and clinician bios

    Where a nervous patient decides to trust you, and where Google looks for YMYL credentials. Full names, letters after them, training, specialisations, memberships, real photographs, and why each clinician does this work.

  • 04

    Location page

    Address, service area, parking, transit, what the building looks like. A serious local signal. One page per location, never a shared one.

  • 05

    FAQ

    Badly underused, and the natural home for every question in category five.

Technical, mostly once

  • Site speed checked in PageSpeed Insights, aiming for 70 or better on mobile
  • Mobile layout confirmed on an actual phone
  • HTTPS active
  • Search Console connected and sitemap submitted
  • Analytics connected
  • No broken links
  • Schema markup in place, LocalBusiness or MedicalBusiness
  • Core Web Vitals passing
  • robots.txt checked, so you are not accidentally blocking Google

Ticking four of these puts you ahead of most practice sites in your market. If the site itself is the problem rather than the settings, that is a build question, not a technical one.

Four

Winning The Map

You are not competing nationally. You are competing inside a few miles for one specialty. That is a fight a small practice can genuinely win.

There is no single ranking, and this changes everything

Ask most owners where they rank and you get one number from a tool that checked once, from one location, probably not yours.

Google answers the same search differently depending on where the person is standing. You might sit second from your own front door, fourth from a mile north, and nowhere at all in the neighbourhood two miles south you would happily treat.

Measured properly, on a grid of points across your radius, visibility has a shape. Usually the shape is: strong where you already are, invisible everywhere else, with proximity doing all the work and nothing else doing any. We will map yours and send it back, and there is no call attached.

Google Business Profile first, before anything

It powers the map pack and it is the single highest-leverage asset in local search. A complete profile has a name, address and phone that match your site exactly, specific categories rather than vague ones, a description written in the language of section two, real photographs of the actual clinic, and a working booking link.

Reviews are the lever most practices leave alone

Zocdoc's own data puts providers with at least ten reviews at roughly three times the monthly bookings of providers with none. The gap between zero and ten is the one worth closing first, and it is closeable in a month.

  • Consistency beats volume. A few every month signals an active practice. Forty in one week signals something else entirely.
  • Respond to all of them. Warmly to the good, calmly to the bad.
  • Stay compliant. Never confirm somebody was a patient, never discuss a condition, not even when the reviewer brought it up first. This is where practices get themselves into genuine trouble, and the risk is in responding, not in asking.

The reason your grateful patients have not left reviews is not unwillingness. It is that nobody asked them in the ten minutes when they felt grateful.

Consistency and citations

Your name, address and phone must match exactly everywhere they appear. "Suite 200" in one place and "Ste 200" in another is a real signal problem. Then build presence in local directories, the chamber, business listings. Each consistent mention adds to Google's confidence that you are a real, established practice.

Five

Becoming The Local Brand, Not Just A Local Result

Everything up to here makes you findable. This section is what makes you the obvious choice once you are found, and it is where practices stop competing on who is closest.

Teach instead of advertise

The most reliable way to demonstrate expertise is to give it away.

An hour in a room teaching forty people about the condition that keeps them awake does something no advertisement can. You are not claiming to be good. You are being observably good while they watch. One sciatica workshop we ran produced 43 attendees, 22 booked appointments and $22,000 in revenue from a single afternoon.

The same principle scales down. Every question you answer properly on your site, in plain language, without making the reader feel stupid, is a small version of the same demonstration. The room version is here.

Claim what is true before somebody else says it first

Here is a positioning move most practices never make.

There are things you do that every good clinic does and none of them talk about. How you screen. What your evaluation actually involves and why it takes as long as it does. What happens if the first approach does not work. The reason you do not just hand everybody the same exercise sheet.

The first practice in your market to explain any of this in vivid detail owns it. Not because it is exclusive, but because whoever says it second looks like they are copying. This is how brands get built out of things that were never secret.

Be the person who tells them the truth

The strongest position available to any local practice is being the one who will say "you do not need us."

Tell somebody their problem is probably a footwear issue and to try that first. Tell them six weeks of a specific exercise is worth trying before they pay you anything. You will lose a handful of appointments and gain something considerably more valuable: the person who trusts your judgment, tells three other people, and comes to you first for the rest of their life.

That is not softness. It is the most durable competitive advantage there is, and it costs nothing.

Six

Turning Visibility Into Booked Evaluations

Getting found is half the job. The path is: search, find you, click, decide you are right, book. Every one of those steps leaks, and every leak is money you already paid to earn.

The arithmetic worth sitting with

A site converting at one percent turns a hundred visitors into one patient. At four percent it turns the same hundred into four.

Same traffic, same spend, four times the schedule. That is the cheapest growth available to you and it requires no new marketing at all.

Make saying yes effortless

  • Online booking is not optional. If somebody cannot book at ten at night, you are losing the person in the most pain.
  • A booking action on every page, above the fold. Not buried behind "Contact."
  • Lead with the low-commitment option if you offer one. A short call or a free screen is a far easier yes than a full evaluation, and it lifts booking rates substantially.
  • Assume a phone. Most people will find you on one.

Build the trust before they have to ask for it

  • A real photograph of the clinician they will actually see.
  • Credentials where they can be found without hunting.
  • Reviews visible on your own site, not only on Google.
  • The objections answered in the copy: what it costs, whether they need a referral, what happens in the first visit, whether you take their plan.

Reverse the risk

Every prospective patient is carrying a private fear that this will be another waste of time and money like the last three things they tried.

Take that risk off them and put it on yourself. A free screen before anybody pays. A written plan they keep whether they book or not. A first visit that ends with an honest answer rather than a package.

The one thing you can say that almost no competitor will: if we cannot help you, we will tell you and point you at who can.

Remove the friction

Slow pages. A phone number that appears on one page. An enquiry that sits until Tuesday.

Respond inside the hour, not the day. The enquiry that arrives on a Friday night and gets answered on Monday morning has usually already been treated somewhere else. This one thing costs nothing and is worth more than most of what you will spend money on. If you do not know how many calls you are missing, start by getting that number.

Seven

Measuring It

You cannot improve what you do not count, and the tools are free.

  • Google Search Console. Which queries reach you, which nearly do, and where you sit for each. The single most useful free tool in this list.
  • Google Analytics. Where visitors arrive from, what they read, where they leave.
  • Your Business Profile insights. Calls, direction requests and website clicks straight from the map.

The only number that actually matters

What a patient is worth across a full plan of care, multiplied by the share of enquiries that become patients.

Most owners have never calculated it, which is exactly why marketing feels like a cost rather than an investment. Multiply those two together and every decision in this guide becomes obvious.

Eight

Your One Next Step

Do not do all of this. You will not, and pretending otherwise is how guides like this end up unread.

Do one thing this week: open your Google Business Profile and look at it as a patient would. Is it claimed? Is the category specific? Are the photographs real, or the stock images from a website build four years ago? Is there a booking link? How many reviews, and when was the most recent?

That is thirty minutes and it is the highest-yield thirty minutes available to you.

Then next week, one service page. Then the week after, ask three patients for a review on the day they told you they felt better.

A year of that quietly compounds into a position your competitors cannot buy their way out of.
