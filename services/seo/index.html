Straight answers

Search And AI, Answered Plainly

Is GEO a real thing or a rebranded buzzword?

Fair question, and the industry has earned your cynicism. Here is the part that is not marketing.

A growing share of searches now end without a click, because the answer appears on the results page or inside an AI assistant. That is measurable and it is not reversing. The practical consequence is that being the source an engine quotes is now worth something separate from being the link it lists. The name for the work is negotiable. The shift is not.

If AI answers the question, does anybody still click through?

Fewer people, yes. But the ones who do click are further along, because the browsing and comparing already happened in the answer.

This is the part most practices get backwards. They watch traffic fall and conclude the channel is dying. Traffic was never the product. Booked evaluations were. A channel that sends you half the visitors and twice the appointments is not a decline, and if you only measure sessions you will cut the wrong thing.

Should we be blogging?

Almost certainly not the way you are imagining it. Nobody in your town is searching for five stretches for a healthy back and then booking a plan of care.

What earns its place is a page that answers a question a patient asks right before they pick up the phone. What does the first visit cost, do I need a referral in this state, how long does this take to fix, do you take my plan. Those are boring to write and they book patients. Volume of posts is a vanity metric that agencies love because it is easy to invoice.

How does this differ from the local SEO service?

Local SEO is about the map and your immediate catchment. This is about the rest of the search result and about the answer engines.

Most clinics need the local work first, because that is where the intent is highest and the distances shortest. This becomes worth doing once the map is handled, or immediately if you serve a wide area, run cash pay services people research before committing, or compete against hospital systems with large content operations.

How do you measure something that does not produce a click?

By whether you appear in the answer, and by what happens to your booked evaluations. We track which queries surface you inside AI results and overviews, alongside conventional rankings and traffic.

Then we hold all of it against one number: new patients who found you through search. Everything else is diagnostic. If the diagnostics move and that number does not, the diagnostics were wrong.

Our website is fine. Does the technical work really matter?

It matters exactly as much as your foundation matters, which is to say invisibly until it does not hold.

If a crawler cannot read your pages quickly and understand what they describe, everything built on top underperforms, and an AI engine that cannot parse you will quote whoever it can. This is not the part that wins you patients. It is the part that stops the rest of the work from being wasted.
