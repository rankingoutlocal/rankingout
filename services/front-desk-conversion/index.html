Front Desk Conversion

Every Call Answered, Day Or Night

An AI receptionist that picks up on the first ring, answers what patients actually ask, and books them straight into your schedule. At 6am, at lunch, and at 9pm on a Sunday.

You have spent real money getting the phone to ring. Search, ads, referrals, reviews. All of it exists to produce one event: a person with back pain dialling your number. And then one human being at a front desk is expected to catch every one of those calls while also checking a patient in, running an authorization, and rescheduling a cancellation.

They cannot. Nobody could. So calls go to voicemail, and here is what makes that expensive: a patient in pain does not leave a message and wait. They hang up and dial the next clinic on the list. You never learn the call happened.

You do not need an industry statistic to size this. Take what one new patient is worth over a full plan of care, and multiply it by the calls that went unanswered last week. That number is the actual cost of your phone, and it is almost always larger than anyone in the practice expects.

What it does

A Receptionist That Never Misses, Never Sleeps

Not a voicemail box and not a phone tree. A voice agent trained on your practice that holds a real conversation, handles the routine calls end to end, and knows exactly when to put a human on the line.

01

Answers Every Call

First ring, every time, including when three people call at once. No hold music, no busy signal, no "please leave a message after the tone."

02

Books Into Your Schedule

Checks real availability and puts the appointment on the calendar your front desk already works from. The patient hangs up with a confirmed time, not a promise of a callback.

03

Answers What They Actually Ask

Do you take my insurance. Do I need a referral. Where do I park. How long is the first visit. What should I wear. The questions your team answers forty times a week.

Name, number, reason for calling, and how they found you, logged against the campaign that produced them, so you finally know which marketing makes the phone ring.

05

Hang-ups, wrong-hours calls, and anyone who did not book get an immediate text and a follow-up attempt. A missed call becomes a second chance instead of a dead end.

06

Hands Off To A Human

Anything clinical, sensitive, or simply beyond it goes to your team with the context already gathered, not passed over cold.

Where we draw the line

It Books Appointments. It Does Not Practice.

The fastest way to get an AI receptionist badly wrong in healthcare is to let it start answering clinical questions. We build the boundary in deliberately, because the wrong answer at 9pm is not a customer service problem.

The rule we build to

No triage. No advice. No judgement calls about symptoms.

Your agent schedules, informs, and captures. It does not tell a caller whether their numbness is serious, whether they should keep exercising, or whether they can skip the imaging their physician ordered.

Red-flag language routes to a person immediately, and where a caller describes something urgent, they are directed to appropriate care rather than offered a slot three days out. Every clinic has its own list of what those triggers are. We build yours in before the agent takes a single call.

The same discipline applies to what the call produces. A patient describing their symptoms over the phone is handing you protected health information, so recordings, transcripts, and everything downstream are set up on that basis, with agreements in place, and nothing quietly copied into an analytics or advertising tool along the way.

Straight answers

Missed Calls, Answered Plainly

Will patients know they are not talking to a person?

Most will work it out, and it matters far less than owners expect. What people react badly to is not being answered by a system. It is being trapped in one.

Answered on the first ring at seven in the evening, asked sensible questions, and booked, beats ringing out to voicemail every single time. The comparison is never a system versus your best receptionist on a quiet morning. It is a system versus nobody.

What happens when somebody calls with something urgent or clinical?

It routes to a person immediately. That behaviour is configured before anything goes live, and it is the first thing we set up rather than the last.

Anything resembling a clinical question, a red flag symptom, or distress leaves the automation and reaches your team. The system is there to handle scheduling, questions about insurance and hours, and the calls nobody was answering anyway. It is not there to make clinical judgements and should never be asked to.

Are we replacing our front desk with this?

No, and the practices that try it regret it. Your front desk is doing things no system does: reading the room, calming a nervous new patient, catching the thing that was not said.

What this removes is the part of their job that was making them worse at the rest of it. Answering the phone mid-check-in, at lunch, during a treatment. Take that away and the same team converts more of the calls that matter.

Is this HIPAA safe?

A caller describing symptoms is handing you protected health information, so the recordings, transcripts and everything downstream have to be set up on that basis from the beginning.

That means agreements in place with anyone processing it, and nothing quietly copied into a marketing tool because it was convenient. Ask any vendor who will sign a business associate agreement and where the recordings live. If the answer is vague, that is your answer.

How many calls are we actually missing?

You do not know, and neither does anybody else, which is precisely the problem worth solving first.

Practices consistently underestimate this because a missed call leaves no trace. It does not appear in a report, nobody complains, and the patient simply calls the next clinic. Before you buy anything, get the number. If it turns out to be two a week, do not buy this. If it is twenty, the arithmetic makes itself.

What if it books the wrong appointment type?

Then it is misconfigured, and that is a setup failure rather than a technology one. The agent is built on your specifics: your services, hours, locations, providers, intake process and the plans you accept.

It gets tuned from real calls, continuously, because the questions patients ask change. Anybody who installs one of these and walks away has sold you a product rather than done the work.
